# NIRDHOOM research notes: Punjab crop-residue management

_Last reviewed: 27 September 2026_

This note separates public-sector observations from NIRDHOOM product assumptions. The figures below are contextual state-level references; they are not NIRDHOOM activity, forecasts, or guarantees.

## Product implications for NIRDHOOM

1. **A machine count is not a service promise.** A booking should depend on machine identity, current availability, operational status, travel distance, capacity, and a credible service window.
2. **Separate in-situ and ex-situ choices.** In-situ operations retain/manage residue in the field; ex-situ pathways collect and move material. They have different equipment, logistics, and buyer requirements.
3. **Treat residue demand as conditional.** Buyers may require moisture, bale dimensions, contamination limits, delivery distance, and a contracted quantity. Avoid presenting a listed buyer or pathway as a confirmed offtake until a contract exists.
4. **Make evidence layered.** A farmer/operator receipt, geotagged evidence, field boundary provenance, and satellite observations answer different questions. A missing satellite fire detection is not proof that no burning occurred; a fire point alone does not prove that a specific field burned.
5. **Keep demo and public data visibly separate.** State statistics belong in a dated research/context panel. Demo farmer, machine, booking, price, and payment records must not be presented as live operational outcomes.
6. **Do not turn policy figures into user promises.** Scheme subsidies, machine availability, prices, clearance deadlines, carbon credits, and payouts require current eligibility, local capacity, and contractual/provider verification.

## Limitations and refresh policy

- Every number should be displayed with its source, period, geography, and whether it is an estimate, target, or observed count.
- Recheck official scheme terms and season counts before each harvest season.
- Do not infer district-level demand or machine availability from state totals.
- This note is a research baseline, not agronomic advice, a legal interpretation, or a commercial feasibility study.
