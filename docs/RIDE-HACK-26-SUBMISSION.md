# RIDE Hack '26 — NIRDHOOM Submission Brief

## Positioning

**Project:** NIRDHOOM  
**One-liner:** A field-first digital coordination layer that connects farmers, residue-removal machinery, field evidence and downstream parali pathways.

**Startup framing:** NIRDHOOM turns fragmented crop-residue clearance into a traceable rural operations workflow without requiring the software company to own the machinery fleet.

## Why it fits RIDE

JIIT Innovation describes RIDE Hack as a real-world technology and innovation challenge with a route toward startup development and incubation. NIRDHOOM is intentionally framed as a product/startup opportunity rather than only a dashboard.

Official event page: https://www.innovationjiit.in/

## Problem

Crop-residue management is a short seasonal operations problem. A farmer needs a practical answer to:

1. Which field is being handled?
2. Who is responsible for the pickup or in-field operation?
3. When is the machine expected?
4. What evidence shows the job happened?
5. What happens to the collected residue next?

Existing machinery and downstream pathways can exist while coordination remains fragmented across calls, spreadsheets and disconnected records.

## Solution

NIRDHOOM connects:

**Field → farmer consent → booking → machine/dispatch → GPS & evidence → verification → residue lot → buyer/offtake pathway**

The product deliberately separates:
- farmer-facing actions;
- operator actions;
- dispatch decisions;
- verification evidence;
- commercial offtake.

## What is demonstrated

- Farmer-first mobile workflow
- Field records and field search
- Server-owned booking boundary
- Dispatch/route workflow
- Operator GPS and evidence primitives
- Offline evidence queue
- Layered verification model
- Telegram farmer channel and secure account linking
- Residue pooling and buyer-demand foundation
- Research/impact workspace
- Responsive mobile UX
- Production-style authentication/security boundaries

## What is not claimed

- No live commercial fleet is claimed.
- No buyer contract is claimed.
- No field-pilot outcome is claimed.
- Satellite observations are not treated as standalone no-burn proof.
- Real payment/payout movement is disabled in this release.
- Carbon credits are not issued by the prototype.

## Startup model to test

The first commercial hypothesis is a transparent coordination/service fee around successful clearance workflows.

Separate future hypotheses:
- verified residue handling/offtake;
- software/API services for organizations coordinating machinery;
- evidence and reporting services.

Carbon and financial products are **not** part of the current revenue claim.

## Competitive advantage

NIRDHOOM is not trying to win by owning more hardware.

The proposed advantage is the operational record connecting:
**who requested → which field → which machine → what happened → what evidence exists → what residue pathway follows.**

That record can become the coordination layer for a distributed machinery network.

## Application copy

### Short description

> NIRDHOOM is a field-first crop-residue coordination platform connecting farmers, machinery operators, dispatch, evidence and residue offtake. It turns a fragmented seasonal problem into a traceable workflow: field → booking → machine → proof → parali.

### Innovation

> Instead of treating stubble management as only a mapping or awareness problem, NIRDHOOM models the operational chain. A farmer can request a field service, operators can capture GPS/evidence, dispatch can coordinate machinery, and verified residue can move into downstream demand pathways. The system is designed to work over existing machinery rather than requiring a new hardware fleet.

### Scalability

> The workflow is geography-aware but not geography-dependent. The same field/booking/operator/evidence/residue model can be adapted to other regions and residue-management systems after local validation of machinery, agronomy, field-boundary and commercial requirements.

### Incubation ask

> We want to validate NIRDHOOM with a supervised field pilot, refine the farmer experience with local users, connect verified machinery capacity, validate residue offtake economics, and measure operational outcomes against a defined baseline.
